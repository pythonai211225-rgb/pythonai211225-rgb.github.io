Resampling Time Series

After a datetime column becomes the index, resample() groups rows by time periods such as months, quarters, or years.

Merge (Joins)

pd.merge() combines two DataFrames by matching rows on a shared key column, exactly like SQL joins. The how parameter controls which rows are kept.

Sample data
# Create the first DataFrame (Employees) df_employees = pd.DataFrame({ 'emp_id': [1, 2, 3, 4, 5], 'name': ['Alice', 'Bob', 'Charlie', 'David', 'Eva'], 'dept_id': [10, 20, 10, 30, 99] # 99 does not exist in departments }) # Create the second DataFrame (Departments) df_departments = pd.DataFrame({ 'dept_id': [10, 20, 30, 40, 50], 'dept_name': ['HR', 'Engineering', 'Marketing', 'Sales', 'Finance'] }) display(df_employees) display(df_departments)
df_employees: emp_id name dept_id 0 1 Alice 10 1 2 Bob 20 2 3 Charlie 10 3 4 David 30 4 5 Eva 99 df_departments: dept_id dept_name 0 10 HR 1 20 Engineering 2 30 Marketing 3 40 Sales 4 50 Finance
1. Inner Join — only matching rows
df_inner = pd.merge(df_employees, df_departments, on='dept_id', how='inner') # If key column names differ: left_on='dept_id', right_on='id' df_inner
emp_id name dept_id dept_name 0 1 Alice 10 HR 1 3 Charlie 10 HR 2 2 Bob 20 Engineering 3 4 David 30 Marketing

Eva (dept_id 99) is dropped — no match in departments. Depts 40 and 50 are also dropped.

2. Left Join — keep all rows from the left DataFrame
df_left = pd.merge(df_employees, df_departments, on='dept_id', how='left') df_left
emp_id name dept_id dept_name 0 1 Alice 10 HR 1 2 Bob 20 Engineering 2 3 Charlie 10 HR 3 4 David 30 Marketing 4 5 Eva 99 NaN

Eva is kept with NaN for dept_name because dept_id 99 has no match on the right.

3. Right Join — keep all rows from the right DataFrame
df_right = pd.merge(df_employees, df_departments, on='dept_id', how='right') df_right
emp_id name dept_id dept_name 0 1.0 Alice 10 HR 1 3.0 Charlie 10 HR 2 2.0 Bob 20 Engineering 3 4.0 David 30 Marketing 4 NaN NaN 40 Sales 5 NaN NaN 50 Finance

Depts 40 and 50 are kept with NaN for emp_id and name because they have no employees. emp_id becomes float because it now holds NaN.

4. Outer Join — keep all rows from both DataFrames
df_outer = pd.merge(df_employees, df_departments, on='dept_id', how='outer') df_outer
emp_id name dept_id dept_name 0 1.0 Alice 10 HR 1 3.0 Charlie 10 HR 2 2.0 Bob 20 Engineering 3 4.0 David 30 Marketing 4 5.0 Eva 99 NaN 5 NaN NaN 40 Sales 6 NaN NaN 50 Finance

Full outer join keeps every row from both sides. Unmatched cells are filled with NaN.

5. Cross Join — every row paired with every other row
df_cross = pd.merge(df_employees, df_departments, how='cross') df_cross.head(10)
emp_id name dept_id_x dept_id_y dept_name 0 1 Alice 10 10 HR 1 1 Alice 10 20 Engineering 2 1 Alice 10 30 Marketing 3 1 Alice 10 40 Sales 4 1 Alice 10 50 Finance 5 2 Bob 20 10 HR 6 2 Bob 20 20 Engineering 7 2 Bob 20 30 Marketing 8 2 Bob 20 40 Sales 9 2 Bob 20 50 Finance

Cross join produces every possible combination: 5 employees × 5 departments = 25 rows total. No on key is needed.

Handling duplicate column names with suffixes

When both DataFrames share a column name that is not the join key, pandas appends suffixes to distinguish them.

orders = pd.DataFrame({ "order_id": [101, 102], "date": ["2026-06-01", "2026-06-02"], "status": ["Paid", "Paid"], "test": [1, 2] }) shipping = pd.DataFrame({ "order_id": [101, 102], "date": ["2026-06-03", "2026-06-05"], "status": ["Shipped", "Delayed"] }) final_report = pd.merge( orders, shipping, on="order_id", suffixes=("_order", "_ship") ) display(final_report)
order_id date_order status_order test date_ship status_ship 0 101 2026-06-01 Paid 1 2026-06-03 Shipped 1 102 2026-06-02 Paid 2 2026-06-05 Delayed

Both DataFrames had date and status columns. The suffixes argument renames them to date_order / date_ship and status_order / status_ship.

Export DataFrames

Pandas can write the same DataFrame to different file formats.

# Create a small dictionary that will become a DataFrame. data = { 'Date': ['2025-04-01', '2025-04-01', '2025-04-02'], 'Product': ['Apples', 'Oranges', 'Apples'], 'Sales': [100, 150, 180] } # Convert the dictionary into a pandas DataFrame. df = pd.DataFrame(data) # Export the DataFrame as an HTML table. df.to_html('html1.html') # Export the DataFrame as a CSV file. df.to_csv('csv1.csv') # Export the DataFrame as an Excel file. df.to_excel('ex1.xlsx')
For to_excel(), you may need an Excel writer package such as openpyxl installed in your Python environment.

Pivot Tables

A pivot table summarizes values by rows and columns. It is perfect for cross-tab style questions.
